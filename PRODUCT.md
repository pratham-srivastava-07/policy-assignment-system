# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Warp's policy assignment workspace serves company and HR administrators who manage employee policy state throughout the working day. They investigate why an employee has a policy, preview the consequences of employee or rule changes, create and review assignment rules, manage exceptions, inspect history, and confirm that reconciliation completed. Employee and manager self-service are outside the MVP.

## Product Purpose

Warp makes deterministic workforce-policy decisions understandable, previewable, and auditable. It connects employee context and explicit decisions to the policies that apply, preserves effective-dated history, and shows administrators both why an assignment exists and what will change before an important edit is saved.

Success means an administrator can confidently answer: which policy applies, why it won, when it became effective, what changed, and whether the resulting assignments have been reconciled.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, and trustworthy. The product should feel like serious infrastructure software adapted for People Operations: operationally dense when the task requires it, restrained in presentation, and plain-spoken about consequences and limitations.

## Anti-references

- Generic HR CRUD dashboards that hide policy reasoning behind forms and tables.
- HR marketing-site styling inside the authenticated product.
- Decorative SaaS patterns such as giant KPI cards, gradients, ornamental motion, excessive rounding, and AI sparkle motifs.
- Interfaces that imply unsupported behavior, hide consequential outcomes in transient messages, or use color as the only status signal.
- Direct visual imitation of reference products; Stripe, Linear, and Ramp are quality benchmarks, not templates.

## Design Principles

1. Make explainability and previewability first-class product primitives.
2. Show computed consequences before assignment-affecting actions are committed.
3. State backend limitations and asynchronous behavior directly.
4. Keep workflows familiar and efficient for non-engineering administrators.
5. Preserve historical context, permission boundaries, and status meaning throughout the interface.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG AA contrast targets: at least 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for UI boundaries. Support complete keyboard navigation, visible focus, logical heading and tab order, semantic controls and tables, properly labelled focus-managed dialogs, reduced motion, and status communication that never relies on color alone.
